Translation that stays on the page

Reading a screen in another language usually means leaving it for a pop-up or a side panel. Mwoyeo paints the translation back in place, in the same size and colour.

The patch has to cover exactly the old glyphs and nothing else, the new text has to fit the room the old text had, it has to look like the page it sits on, and it has to stay put when the page moves. Mwoyeo does this for the area under a movable lens.

The last requirement is the one that separates a demonstration from a tool. A lens over a chat or a feed is used while the person scrolls it, and every stage works on a frame that the display has already left behind.

Left alone, the translations trail the text by the speed of the scroll times the age of the frame; the eye reads that as a shimmer, and in the worst case a translation sits on the wrong line.

A scroll predictor places each translation where the content will be by the time it is shown, from wheel and trackpad events alone, and learns how far one wheel unit moves the page under the pointer.

Note The predictor needs nothing from the page itself: it reads only the wheel events the system already delivers to every window.

Before you start

  • Allow screen recording once
  • Pick the language you read
  • Hold Option to pin a lens to the screen
  • Press Escape to close the picker
