# lens-hold: what a resized lens shows, measured

Round hold117, docs/design/lens-hold.md. Two instruments for one question: while a lens is resized over a page that does not change, does anything it had drawn go away?

1. `resize-hold-checks` (`App/Support/ResizeHoldChecks.swift`, also a part of `check-logic`): a real `RegionSession` on a capture that serves a still page, the corner dragged as `RegionFrameWindow.mouseDragged` does it, a mock engine (`tools/mock-openai`, 120 ms). Every snapshot of the pipeline is kept and compared with the one before it (`judge`): the page's text back under a block that was drawn, a drawn block swapped for another standing over it (`swaps`: the panel fades both at once), no block, `overlay.blanked`. Three parts: hand resizes of one lens; a lens resized while its first read is under way (`pass.rebased`); a narrow lens that is Fitted to the page's width in one jump, Undone, and moved. `MWOYEO_HOLD_TIMELINE=<file>` writes the timeline of part one (one line per snapshot: blocks, untranslated texts, the largest share of the page's text pixels under a block of the snapshot before that is dark again, ink before and after), `<file>.fit` the one of part three, and `<file>.page.png`. With `MWOYEO_TRACE=1` each re-read prints a `carry:` line: the old drawn blocks, the ones the page's change keeps from handing on, and the old blocks no new block holds half of.
2. `record.sh` + `luma.py`: the same run under `screencapture -V`; `luma.py` takes the lens crop that every step of the run keeps and gives, per frame, the share of the page's text pixels that are dark again (about 0.35 while translations are drawn over the erased text, 1 where the page's own text shows) and lists the stretches of up to 2 s above 0.6 as dips: their number, their length, their depth.

Reading: a dip is the page's own text showing where a translation was. On the base (main 3a2e86a + the counters) one resize of the check gives one dip of 142 ms (9 frames) and depth 0.74 (337 frames of 260 s; the mock answers after 120 ms, an engine that takes a second makes the dip a second long); with the fix, none (508 frames). `luma.py` looks at the first lens of part one only: part three is judged on the snapshots.
