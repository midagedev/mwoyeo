The quiet season on the northern coast

By Mara Ellis · 8 min read

When the summer crowds leave, the harbor towns slow to the pace of the tide, and the light turns long and gold.

Most visitors come in July, when every café has a line and the ferries run full. By October the same streets belong to the people who live here again.

The bakery opens an hour later. The ferry captain has time to talk about the weather, which he reads from the color of the water rather than his phone.

Walk the cliff path in the morning and you may not meet anyone at all. The gulls are louder than the cars, and the wind carries the smell of salt and pine.

Plan for short days and long dinners. Many inns close on Mondays, but the ones that stay open will keep a table by the fire for you.
