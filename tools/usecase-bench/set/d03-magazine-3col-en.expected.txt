The Quiet Return of the Paper Map
By Helen Marsh
When the signal drops in the middle of a mountain pass, the glowing blue dot on a phone stops being useful.
Hikers have noticed. Sales of printed maps have risen for the third year in a row, according to the largest publisher in the country.
“People want to see the whole picture,” says one shop owner. “A screen shows you where you are. A map shows you where you could go.”
The trend is not limited to the outdoors. City guides, too, are finding a new audience among travelers tired of notifications.
Still, almost everyone carries both.
